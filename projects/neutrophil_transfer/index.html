Mitochondrial Transfer into Neutrophils

Isolated mitochondria were transferred into granulocytes across several runs. Uptake was low and the activity signal was lost within about 20 minutes. Two causes were identified: the isolation step itself damages mitochondria, and uptake is genetically constrained. A route that avoids isolation is the next experiment.

Objective

  • Deliver functional mitochondria into neutrophils.
  • Keep them active long enough to produce a measurable effect.

Method

  1. Isolate mitochondria from donor cells by differential centrifugation.
  2. Label with a fluorescent activity dye.
  3. Co-incubate with granulocytes.
  4. Follow the fluorescent signal over time.

Results

  • Uptake was low in every run.
  • Transferred mitochondria lost detectable activity after about 20 minutes.
  • Lipid-mediated delivery was the first hypothesis for the loss. It was dropped once the cause became clearer.
  • Negative result: isolated mitochondria are too fragile for this cell type without stabilisation.

What explained it

  1. The isolation protocol in the literature is not the one the lab uses. Less filtration, a different buffer, and sonication rather than a fine needle, all to limit damage. Learned while shadowing the ARCHIE project.
  2. Mitochondrial uptake is genetically limited. This came out of the secondary transfer analysis running in parallel.
  3. The PORTER team keeps mitochondria in blood, stabilises them there, and performs the transfer in that environment rather than extracting them. Their result matches the hypothesis I started from. It is unpublished, so it was not available from the literature.

Status and next steps

  • 19 September. Run scheduled at the Stanford lab, postponed when the professor had to leave town.
  • 22 September. Rescheduled as a variation of the in-blood route.
  • Fallback. Reproduce in Italy. No PORTER machine there, and the mitochondria may not survive without it.
  • Reagent ordering was blocked until grant funds reached the lab account, since ordering requires a lab ID.

Assessment

  • The initial hypothesis about granulocytes was correct, but not testable with the method available at the time.
  • The limiting factor was the preparation, not the cell type.

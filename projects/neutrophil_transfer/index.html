Mitochondrial Transfer into Neutrophils

Between May and June I ran several experiments transferring isolated mitochondria into granulocytes. Uptake stayed low, and the transferred mitochondria lost detectable activity after about 20 minutes. Shadowing an active lab and talking with the PORTER team showed why: isolated mitochondria are very fragile, and uptake is genetically limited. Their approach keeps mitochondria in blood and stabilises them there, which matches what I had hypothesised.

First attempts

Uptake was low in every run. Based on the fluorescent activity assay, the mitochondria that did get in consistently lost signs of activity after roughly 20 minutes. My first idea was that lipid-mediated delivery might fix this.

What changed my mind

While shadowing Coco in the lab (see the ARCHIE shadowing), I learned that mitochondrial extraction is done quite differently from what the published papers suggest. The lab uses a gentler protocol, with less filtration, a different buffer and a different way of breaking the cells open, all to avoid damaging the mitochondria.

In parallel, the secondary transfer analysis suggested that mitochondrial uptake is genetically limited. I discussed this with the PORTER team, who use the PORTER machine to transfer mitochondria into neutrophils. Instead of extracting mitochondria, they keep them in blood, stabilise them to preserve their activity, and do the transfer in that environment.

their result is what I expected, but it isn't published yet, so I couldn't have known from the literature

Results so far

My initial hypothesis about granulocytes was correct, even though I had no way to test it at the time. The useful negative result is that isolated mitochondria seem too fragile for this cell type without stabilisation.

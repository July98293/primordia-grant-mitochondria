Secondary Mitochondrial Transfer

The idea is to use cells as “Uber vehicles” for mitochondria. Instead of transferring isolated mitochondria directly, we identify or engineer cells that can take up mitochondria and deliver them to specific recipient cells. I built a single-cell RNA-seq pipeline across the immune system to find which cells are the best candidates to receive, carry and redistribute mitochondria, and which biological programs drive successful uptake. The strongest candidate is the myeloid progenitor.

Motivation

In principle, one cell could act as a mitochondrial donor or carrier and deliver mitochondria to another cell type. That would allow more controlled and potentially targeted delivery than injecting fragile isolated mitochondria.

Method

I had never worked with single-cell data before. At the start of July I spent about two weeks learning single-cell RNA-seq analysis from zero: data structures, normalisation, pseudobulk analysis, batch structure and cell-state scoring.

I then built the pipeline and reworked it about three times, refining the method and validating the results each round. The pipeline is now final and waiting for review.

Questions the pipeline answers

  1. Which pathways correlate with mitochondrial uptake and transfer?
  2. Which immune cells are strongest at producing, receiving and donating mitochondria?
  3. Which cells can talk to each other, and which immune cells are best at donating to others?
